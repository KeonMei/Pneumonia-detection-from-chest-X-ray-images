Introduction

Why pneumonia detection?

  • Pneumonia is the leading infectious killer of children under five (WHO, 2022)
  • A chest X-ray confirms it — but radiologists are scarce in many regions
  • CNNs already reach expert level on chest X-ray tasks (Kermany et al., 2018; Rajpurkar et al., 2017)
  • Goal: a fast, reproducible second opinion that sorts X-rays so likely pneumonias are read first
  • Course project — not a medical device, not a diagnosis

Research question

How much does a fine-tuned CNN improve pneumonia detection over classical ML on the same data — and how well does it generalise to unseen patients and hospitals?

Discussion

What worked, what limits the model

Deep learning helps

Fine-tuning beats all four baselines on both test sets (AUC 0.944 vs 0.916 on children, 0.802 vs 0.783 on adults). Baselines call most healthy children ill (specificity 0.17–0.31); the CNN clears 70% of them. NIH AUC 0.802 vs 0.768 reported for CheXNet (different label definition).

Domain shift is the limit

94% of training images are adult NIH X-rays → least certain on healthy children (Zech et al., 2018). Missed adult pneumonias: map sits on text markers / corners — shortcut learning. NIH labels are noisy (Oakden-Rayner, 2020); Kermany normals split per image; small test set (1 image = 0.16 pp).
